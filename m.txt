Write the splash wordmark with one wipe, not four

Splitting "Note" into a span per letter clipped each glyph at its own
advance width. A script face draws ink well past that -- which is how its
letters connect -- so every reveal sliced the glyph it was revealing and
let the neighbour's ink through, and the word appeared to write over
itself.

A single left-to-right clip across the whole word fixes it and is closer
to the idea anyway: a pen moving across, rather than four letters taking
turns to appear. The negative insets keep the tail of the "e" and the
descenders whole, and the timing still lands where the old animation did,
so the domain and the stillness before the clear are unchanged.
